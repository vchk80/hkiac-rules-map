Notice of Arbitration (Art 4)

  • Request that the dispute be referred to arbitration.
  • Names and contact details (email, address, phone) of all parties and their representatives.
  • Copy of the arbitration agreements invoked, plus copies of all contracts or other legal instruments related to or referenced in the dispute.
  • General nature of the claim, the amount involved and the relief or remedy sought.
  • Claimant's proposal and any comments regarding the designated arbitrator(s).
  • Disclosure of any funding agreement and the identity of any third party funder.
  • Confirmation that copies of the Notice and supporting materials have been sent to the Respondent simultaneously.
  • Must be accompanied by payment of the Registration Fee; may include the Statement of Claim.

Answer to the Notice (Art 5, within 30 days)

  • Contact information.
  • Any plea objecting that a tribunal under these Rules lacks jurisdiction.
  • Respondent's comments and answer on the nature of the claim, the amount involved and the relief sought.
  • Respondent's proposal and comments regarding the designated arbitrator(s).
  • Disclosure of any funding agreement and third party funder.
  • Confirmation of simultaneous service on the Claimant.
  • May include the Statement of Defence if the Notice included the Statement of Claim.
  • Any counterclaim, set-off defence or cross-claim raised here, with copies of related contracts, a description of the nature and amount, and the relief sought.

Statement of Claim (Art 16)

  • Statement of facts supporting the claim.
  • Points at issue.
  • Legal arguments.
  • Relief or remedy sought.

Statement of Defence (Art 17)

  • Reply to all particulars in the Statement of Claim.
  • If applicable, the legal and factual basis of any objection to jurisdiction or to the proper constitution of the tribunal.
  • If applicable, the same four particulars for a counterclaim, set-off defence or cross-claim.

Notice of Challenge to an Arbitrator (Art 11.8)

  • Sent within 15 days after the confirmation or appointment was communicated to the challenging party, or within 15 days after that party became aware of the circumstances relied on (Art 11.7).
  • Communicated to HKIAC, all other parties, the challenged arbitrator, and any other members of the tribunal.
  • Must state the reasons for the challenge.
  • Grounds under Art 11.6: justifiable doubts as to impartiality or independence; the arbitrator lacks qualifications agreed by the parties; the arbitrator becomes de jure or de facto unable to perform their functions, or fails to act without undue delay.
  • A party may challenge an arbitrator it designated, or in whose appointment it participated, only for reasons of which it became aware after the designation.
  • Unless the arbitrator resigns or the non-challenging party agrees within 15 days of receiving the notice, HKIAC decides the challenge; the tribunal, including the challenged arbitrator, may continue the arbitration meanwhile.

Request for Joinder (Art 27.6)

  • Case reference; contact information.
  • Request that the additional party be joined.
  • Copies of contracts or legal instruments giving the legal basis of the request.
  • Statement of facts; points at issue; legal arguments; relief sought.
  • Existence of any funding agreement and third party funder.
  • Confirmation that full copies have been communicated to all parties.

Answer to the Request for Joinder (Art 27.7, within 15 days)

  • Contact information.
  • Any plea that the tribunal has been improperly constituted or lacks jurisdiction over the additional party.
  • Comments on the particulars set out in the Request; answer to the relief sought.
  • Any additional claims sought; any funding agreement or funder.
  • Confirmation of communication to all parties.
  • An additional party wishing to join files its own Request; all other parties comment within 15 days.

Request for Consolidation (Art 28.3)

  • Case references; contact information.
  • Request that the arbitrations be consolidated.
  • Copy of the arbitration agreement; copies of related contracts and legal instruments.
  • Description of the nature of each claim and the amount involved.
  • Statement of facts and written consent of all parties to consolidate, where relevant.
  • Points at issue; legal arguments.
  • Details of any mandatory provision affecting consolidation.
  • Comments on constitution of the tribunal if the Request is granted.
  • Confirmation of service; non-requesting parties and arbitrators may be asked for comments.

Third party funding disclosure (Art 44)

  • Not a standalone filing, but a mandatory disclosure carried inside the filings above.
  • The funded party must disclose the fact that a funding agreement has been made, and the identity of the third party funder.
  • Made in the emergency arbitrator application, the Notice, the Answer, the Request for Joinder or the Answer to the Request for Joinder, as applicable.
  • If the agreement is made after commencement, disclosure is due as soon as practicable afterwards, and any later changes must also be disclosed.

Application for Emergency Relief (Sch 4 ¶2)

  • Filed before, with, or after the Notice, but before the tribunal is constituted.
  • Party and representative details; the circumstances giving rise to the Application and to the underlying dispute.
  • A statement of the Emergency Relief sought, why it is urgent and cannot await constitution, and why the applicant is entitled to it.
  • Any relevant agreement, in particular the arbitration agreement.
  • Comments on the language, the seat of the Emergency Relief proceedings, and the applicable law.
  • Confirmation that the Application Deposit has been paid.
  • Disclosure of any funding agreement and third party funder under Art 44.
  • Confirmation of simultaneous communication to all other parties, identifying the means of service.
  • The full procedure is set out under category 12, Interim and urgent relief.

Request for Early Determination (Art 43.4)

  • Made as promptly as possible.
  • Request and description of the points of law or fact.
  • Statement of facts; legal arguments.
  • Proposed form of the early determination procedure.
  • Comments on how that proposal meets the efficiency and fairness objectives.
  • Confirmation of simultaneous communication.

Valid means of communication (Art 3.1)

  • To the address, facsimile number or email given by the addressee or its representative in the arbitration.
  • Failing that, to the details specified in any applicable agreement between the parties.
  • Failing that, to any details the addressee holds out to the world at the time.
  • Failing that, to any last known address, facsimile number or email of the addressee.
  • By upload to any secured online repository the parties have agreed to use.
  • Through any other agreed form of electronic communication, subject to approval by HKIAC and the tribunal once constituted.

Where communication cannot be effected (Art 3.2)

  • If, after reasonable efforts, none of the routes in Art 3.1 works, a communication is deemed received if sent to the last known address or facsimile number by a means that provides a record of the attempt.

When receipt is deemed to occur (Arts 3.3, 3.4)

  • On the earliest day the communication is made or uploaded, or attempted under Art 3.2.
  • The date is fixed by local time at the place of receipt, or of the notice of upload.
  • Where there are several recipients, receipt is deemed to occur only when the last intended recipient receives it, or receives notice of the upload.

Copying the tribunal and HKIAC (Art 13.3)

  • Subject to the ex parte restriction in Art 11.5, all written communications between a party and the tribunal must go to all other parties and to HKIAC.

Representatives and changes to them (Arts 13.7, 13.8)

  • Parties may be represented by persons of their choice, subject to Arts 13.5 and 13.9.
  • Representatives' names and contact details go to all other parties, HKIAC, any emergency arbitrator, and the tribunal once constituted.
  • The tribunal, emergency arbitrator or HKIAC may require proof of a representative's authority.
  • After constitution, any proposed change or addition to a party's legal representatives must be communicated promptly to all other parties, the tribunal and HKIAC.

Cross-cutting

  • Nearly every filing under the Rules carries its own confirmation-of-service requirement, identifying the means of service used.

Calculation (Arts 3.5, 3.6)

  • Time runs from the day after receipt or deemed receipt.
  • If the last day is a holiday or non-business day at the place of receipt, it extends to the next business day; intervening holidays still count.
  • HKIAC may amend its own and the Rules' time limits, including expired ones, but not those agreed by the parties or set by the tribunal unless they agree.

Party filings (Arts 5.1, 21, 27.7)

  • Answer to the Notice: 30 days.
  • Answer to a Request for Joinder, and other parties' comments: 15 days.
  • Further written statements: should not exceed 45 days; extensions available even after expiry.

Constitution (Arts 6.1, 7, 8, 10.1, 11.7)

  • Number of arbitrators: agreed before commencement or within 30 days, else HKIAC decides.
  • Sole arbitrator: 30 days from the Notice if agreed pre-commencement; 15 days if agreed later or decided by HKIAC.
  • Three arbitrators: 15-day windows for each designation; presiding arbitrator within 30 days of the second confirmation.
  • Fee method notified within 30 days; challenge within 15 days.

Closure, award and after (Arts 31.1, 31.2, 38, 39, 40)

  • Closure no later than 45 days from the last directed submissions.
  • Award within 3 months of closure, extendable by agreement or by HKIAC.
  • Correction, interpretation or additional award: requested within 30 days of receipt; additional award made within 60 days.

Payments and tracks (Arts 41.4, 42.2, 43.5, Sch 4 ¶¶4, 12, 18, 21)

  • Deposits: 30 days from request.
  • Expedited award: 6 months from file transmission, extendable in appropriate circumstances.
  • Early determination: decision in 30 days, order or award in 60 days.
  • Emergency: appointment sought within 24 hours; decision in 14 days; Notice within 7 days of the Application; decision lapses if no tribunal within 90 days.

Registration Fee (Sch 1 ¶1)

  • Non-refundable, submitted with the Notice, in the amount stated on HKIAC's website; otherwise the arbitration will not proceed.

Administrative Fees (Sch 1 ¶2)

  • In accordance with the website schedule as at the date of the Notice.
  • Claims and counterclaims are added to determine the amount in dispute, as are set-off defences and cross-claims unless the tribunal determines otherwise.
  • Interest claims are not taken into account; with alternative claims only the principal claim is counted.
  • HKIAC may depart from the scheduled fees in exceptional circumstances, and fixes a figure where the amount is unquantified.
  • Parties are jointly and severally liable.

Deposits (Art 41)

  • Claimant and Respondent each put down an equal deposit after the Notice.
  • Separate deposits for counterclaims or cross-claims; supplementary deposits may be required throughout.
  • If unpaid within 30 days, HKIAC informs the parties so one may pay; if still unpaid, the procedure may be suspended, ceased or terminated depending on whether the tribunal is constituted.
  • A party paying for another may request an award for reimbursement.
  • On release of the final award, any unexpended balance is returned in the shares paid, or as the tribunal directs.

Tribunal fees (Art 10, Sch 2 and 3)

  • Either hourly rates under Schedule 2 or the sum-in-dispute scale under Schedule 3, agreed within 30 days of the Notice, defaulting to Schedule 2.
  • Hourly rates run from confirmation to final award or order, must be agreed before confirmation, and cannot exceed the published cap.
  • An arbitrator may increase the agreed rate by up to 10% on each anniversary; higher rates only by agreement of all parties or HKIAC in exceptional circumstances.
  • Travel: 50% of the rate for time travelling but not working, 100% for work done while travelling.
  • Under Schedule 3 the scale figure is the maximum payable, with the same aggregation, interest and alternative-claim rules.
  • Parties are jointly and severally liable regardless of who appointed whom; HKIAC and the tribunal hold a lien over the award.

Fee adjustments triggered by procedural steps (Arts 18.2, 27.15, 28.10, 30.2)

  • Art 18.2: HKIAC may adjust its Administrative Fees and the tribunal's fees where a party amends its claim or defence.
  • Art 27.15: HKIAC may adjust both after a Request for Joinder has been submitted.
  • Art 28.10: HKIAC may adjust both after a Request for Consolidation has been submitted.
  • Art 30.2: HKIAC may adjust both where arbitrations are conducted concurrently or consecutively under Art 30.1.
  • Under Sch 1 ¶2.5, these provisions also allow HKIAC to depart from the published fee table when calculating Administrative Fees.
  • Under Sch 3 ¶6.6, they allow the tribunal's fees to exceed the sum-in-dispute scale.

Cancellation and postponement (Sch 2 ¶10)

  • Requested by the tribunal: no charge.
  • By a party 30 days or less before the first booked day: 75% of eight times the hourly rate, per day.
  • By a party between 30 and 60 days before: 50% of eight times the hourly rate, per day.
  • By a party more than 60 days before: no charge.
  • If the arbitrator worked during booked days, the higher of hourly fees or the cancellation fee applies.
  • Cancellation other than by agreement or at the tribunal's request may be weighed in the later apportionment of costs.

Application Deposit for Emergency Relief (Sch 4 ¶5)

  • Set by HKIAC as published on the date the Application is submitted, comprising HKIAC's emergency administrative fees and the emergency arbitrator's fees and expenses.
  • Payment must be confirmed within the Application itself; HKIAC will not proceed without it.
  • The emergency arbitrator is paid hourly under Schedule 2, capped at the published amount unless the parties agree otherwise or HKIAC decides otherwise in exceptional circumstances.
  • Additional deposits may be requested at any time; failure to pay within HKIAC's time limit means the Application is dismissed.

Number (Art 6)

  • HKIAC decides one or three if the parties cannot agree before commencement or within 30 days of the Notice.

Sole arbitrator (Art 7)

  • Agreed before commencement: joint designation within 30 days of the Notice.
  • Agreed after commencement: joint designation within 15 days of that agreement.
  • Decided by HKIAC: joint designation within 15 days of receiving the decision, failing which HKIAC appoints.

Three arbitrators (Art 8)

  • Agreed before commencement: one designation each, named in the Notice and the Answer, failing which HKIAC appoints.
  • Agreed after commencement: Claimant designates within 15 days of the agreement, Respondent within 15 days of that designation.
  • The two designate a presiding arbitrator; if not within 30 days of the second confirmation, HKIAC appoints.
  • More than two parties: the same procedure applies, but any failure to designate, or disagreement over the two-sides premise, may result in HKIAC appointing all three.

Replacement of an arbitrator (Art 12)

  • Where an arbitrator dies, is successfully challenged, has their appointment revoked, is otherwise removed, or resigns, a substitute is appointed under the rules that applied to the appointment of the arbitrator being replaced.
  • Those rules apply even if, during the original appointment, a party failed to exercise its right to designate or participate.
  • This is subject to Arts 12.2, 27.13 and 28.8, the joinder and consolidation provisions under which HKIAC may instead appoint the whole tribunal.
  • On a party's request, and in view of exceptional circumstances, HKIAC may deprive a party of its right to designate a substitute, after giving the parties and remaining arbitrators an opportunity to express their views.
  • In that case HKIAC may appoint the substitute itself, or authorise the remaining arbitrators to proceed and make any decision or award as a truncated tribunal.
  • Where an arbitrator is replaced, the arbitration resumes at the stage where that arbitrator was replaced or ceased to perform their functions, unless the tribunal decides otherwise.
  • HKIAC decides what fees and expenses are payable for a replaced arbitrator's services, taking into account the applicable fee method, the work done and the complexity of the subject-matter (Sch 2 ¶5.2; Sch 3 ¶5).

Confirmation, diversity and challenge (Arts 9, 9A, 11, 27.12, 28.8, 29.2)

  • All designations take effect only on HKIAC confirmation, which considers agreed qualifications, factors affecting efficiency or integrity, impartiality and independence, and availability.
  • Diversity is a stated consideration for parties, co-arbitrators and HKIAC.
  • Notice of challenge within 15 days, stating reasons, communicated to HKIAC, all parties, the challenged arbitrator and the rest of the tribunal.
  • Joinder (Art 27.12), consolidation (Art 28.8) and multi-contract claims (Art 29.2) each trigger a deemed waiver of the right to designate an arbitrator, and HKIAC may revoke confirmations and appoint the tribunal itself.

Procedural discretion (Arts 13.1, 13.5, 13.6)

  • The tribunal adopts suitable procedures to avoid unnecessary delay or expense, having regard to the complexity of the issues, the amount in dispute, the effective use of technology, information security, and environmental impact.
  • Procedures must ensure equal treatment and give each party a reasonable opportunity to present its case.
  • The tribunal and the parties must do everything necessary to ensure the fair and efficient conduct of the arbitration.
  • The tribunal may determine preliminary issues that could dispose of all or part of the case, bifurcate, run the arbitration in sequential stages, and decide at what stage any issue is determined.

Timetable and tribunal secretary (Arts 13.2, 13.4)

  • At an early stage, and after consulting the parties, the tribunal prepares a provisional timetable and provides it to the parties and HKIAC.
  • The tribunal may appoint a secretary after consulting the parties.
  • The secretary must be and remain impartial and independent, must disclose anything likely to give rise to justifiable doubts before appointment, and must disclose without delay any such circumstances arising afterwards.

Representation, conflicts and integrity (Arts 13.9, 13.10)

  • The tribunal may, after consulting the parties, take any measure necessary to avoid a conflict of interest arising from a change in party representation, including excluding the proposed new representatives from participating.
  • HKIAC may, after consulting the parties and the tribunal, take any measure necessary to preserve the efficiency or integrity of the arbitration.
  • That includes, in exceptional circumstances, revoking an arbitrator's appointment where HKIAC considers the arbitrator prevented from or failing to fulfil their functions under the Rules or within the prescribed time limits.

Suspension for settlement talks (Art 13.11)

  • Where the parties agree to pursue other means of settling their dispute after commencement, HKIAC, the tribunal or an emergency arbitrator may suspend the arbitration on such terms as it considers appropriate, at any party's request.
  • The arbitration resumes at the request of any party.

Seat, venue and language (Arts 14, 15)

  • The parties may agree the seat; absent agreement the seat is Hong Kong, unless the tribunal determines another seat is more appropriate having regard to the circumstances.
  • The tribunal may meet at any location outside the seat for consultation, hearing witnesses, experts or the parties, or inspection; the arbitration is still treated for all purposes as conducted at the seat.
  • Absent prior agreement on language, parties communicate in English or Chinese until the tribunal determines the language promptly after constitution.
  • The tribunal may order translations of supporting materials into the language of the arbitration.

Amendments and further statements (Arts 18, 20)

  • A party may amend or supplement its claim or defence during the arbitration, unless the tribunal considers it inappropriate in the circumstances.
  • A claim or defence may not be amended so that it falls outside the tribunal's jurisdiction.
  • HKIAC may adjust its Administrative Fees and the tribunal's fees where a party amends its claim or defence (Art 18.2).
  • The tribunal decides what further written statements are required beyond the Statement of Claim and Statement of Defence, and sets the time limits for them.

Burden, admissibility and weight (Arts 22.1, 22.2)

  • Each party bears the burden of proving the facts relied on to support its claim or defence.
  • The tribunal determines the admissibility, relevance, materiality and weight of the evidence, including whether to apply strict rules of evidence.

Document production (Art 22.3)

  • At any time during the arbitration the tribunal may allow or require a party to produce documents, exhibits or other evidence it determines to be relevant to the case and material to its outcome.
  • The tribunal has the power to admit or exclude any documents, exhibits or other evidence.

Whether to hold hearings (Art 22.4)

  • The tribunal decides whether to hold hearings for evidence or oral argument, or to proceed solely on documents and other materials.
  • It must hold hearings at an appropriate stage if a party so requests or if it considers fit.
  • Where there is a hearing, the parties must be given adequate advance notice of the date, time and place.

Conduct of hearings (Arts 22.5, 22.6, 22.7)

  • The tribunal may determine the manner in which a witness or expert is examined.
  • It may direct translation of oral statements made at a hearing, and a record of the hearing, where necessary in the circumstances.
  • Hearings are held in private unless the parties agree otherwise, and the tribunal may require any witness or expert to leave the hearing room at any time.

Tribunal-appointed experts (Art 25)

  • The tribunal may, after consulting the parties, appoint one or more experts to report in writing on specific issues it determines.
  • It establishes the expert's terms of reference after consulting the parties, and communicates a copy to the parties and HKIAC.
  • The parties must give the expert any relevant information and produce relevant documents or goods; disputes over relevance go to the tribunal.
  • On receipt of the report, the tribunal sends a copy to the parties, who may express their opinions on it and examine any document the expert relied on.
  • At either party's request the expert must attend a hearing where the parties may examine them and present their own experts on the points at issue.
  • Article 11 applies by analogy to any tribunal-appointed expert.

Claimant's failure to file (Art 26.1)

  • If the Claimant fails to communicate its written statement within the tribunal's time limit, without showing sufficient cause, the tribunal may terminate the arbitration.
  • Exception: where another party has brought a claim and wishes the arbitration to continue, the tribunal may proceed in respect of that other party's claim.

Respondent's failure to file (Art 26.2)

  • If the Respondent fails to communicate its written statement within the tribunal's time limit, without showing sufficient cause, the tribunal may proceed with the arbitration notwithstanding that failure.

Failure to present a case (Art 26.3)

  • If a duly notified party fails to present its case in accordance with the Rules or as directed, without showing sufficient cause, the tribunal may proceed and make an award on the basis of the evidence before it.

Non-payment of deposits (Art 41.4)

  • If deposits are not paid in full within 30 days of the request, HKIAC informs the parties so that one of them may pay.
  • If payment is still not made and the tribunal is not yet constituted, HKIAC may suspend or cease to administer the arbitration.
  • Once the tribunal is constituted, it may order suspension or termination, or continue on such basis and in respect of such claim or counterclaim as it considers fit.

Deemed waiver of the right to object (Art 32.1)

  • A party that knows, or ought reasonably to know, that a provision of or requirement under the Rules including the arbitration agreement has not been complied with, and yet proceeds without promptly stating its objection, is deemed to have waived its right to object.

Competence-competence and separability (Arts 19.1, 19.2)

  • The tribunal may rule on its own jurisdiction under the Rules, including objections as to the existence, validity or scope of the arbitration agreement.
  • It has the power to determine the existence or validity of any contract of which an arbitration agreement forms part.
  • An arbitration agreement forming part of a contract is treated as independent of the contract's other terms.
  • A decision that the contract is null and void does not necessarily entail the invalidity of the arbitration agreement.

Timing of pleas (Art 19.3)

  • A plea that the tribunal lacks jurisdiction is to be raised if possible in the Answer, and no later than the Statement of Defence, or in the Defence to Counterclaim.
  • Designating or participating in the appointment of an arbitrator does not preclude a party from raising such a plea.
  • A plea that the tribunal is exceeding the scope of its authority must be raised as soon as the matter arises in the arbitration.
  • The tribunal may admit a later plea in either case if it considers the delay justified.

Questions arising before constitution (Arts 19.4, 19.5, 19.6)

  • Where a question arises before constitution as to the existence, validity or scope of the arbitration agreement, whether claims were properly brought in a single arbitration under Art 29, or HKIAC's competence to administer, the arbitration proceeds and the tribunal decides the question once constituted.
  • The arbitration proceeds only if and to the extent HKIAC is satisfied, prima facie, that an arbitration agreement under the Rules may exist, or that the arbitration was properly commenced under Art 29.
  • HKIAC's prima facie decision is without prejudice to the admissibility or merits of any party's claim or defence.

Joinder and emergency relief (Art 27.2, Sch 4 ¶10)

  • A decision to allow joinder under Art 27.1 is without prejudice to the tribunal's power to decide any question as to its jurisdiction arising from that decision.
  • An emergency arbitrator has the power to rule on objections that it has no jurisdiction, including objections as to the existence, validity or scope of the arbitration clause or agreement, and resolves any dispute over the applicability of Schedule 4.

Joinder (Art 27)

  • Raised no later than the Statement of Defence, save in exceptional circumstances.
  • Available where the additional party is prima facie bound by the arbitration agreement, or where all parties including that party expressly agree.
  • Commencement against the additional party runs from receipt of the Request.
  • Once joined, all parties waive the right to designate an arbitrator; HKIAC may revoke appointments and appoint the tribunal itself. Fees are adjusted accordingly.

Consolidation (Art 28)

  • Available where the parties agree, or all claims are under the same agreement, or there is a common question of law or fact, related transactions and compatible agreements.
  • Arbitrations consolidate into the first-commenced unless the parties agree or HKIAC decides otherwise.
  • The same waiver and revocation consequences follow as for joinder.

Costs where arbitrations are consolidated (Art 34.5)

  • The tribunal in the consolidated arbitration determines the costs of the arbitration in accordance with Arts 34.1 to 34.4.
  • Those costs include the fees of any arbitrator designated, confirmed or appointed in an arbitration that was subsequently consolidated into another.
  • They also include any other costs incurred in that earlier arbitration before consolidation.
  • Revocation of an appointment on consolidation does not affect the arbitrator's entitlement to be paid under Schedule 2 or 3 (Art 28.9(b)).

Multiple contracts and concurrent proceedings (Arts 29, 30, 32.2)

  • Claims under different contracts may be brought in one arbitration where a common question arises under each agreement, the relief arises from the same or related transactions, and the agreements are compatible.
  • Two or more arbitrations may run concurrently or consecutively, or one be suspended, where the same tribunal is constituted in each and a common question arises.
  • Parties waive objections to enforcement based on the use of these procedures, so far as such waiver is valid.

What interim measures can do (Arts 23.2, 23.3)

  • Maintain or restore the status quo pending determination of the dispute.
  • Require action, or restraint from action, likely to cause current or imminent harm or prejudice to the arbitral process.
  • Preserve assets out of which an award may be satisfied.
  • Preserve relevant and material evidence.

Factors (Art 23.4)

  • Harm not adequately reparable by damages is likely if the measure is not ordered.
  • That harm substantially outweighs the likely harm to the party against whom the measure is directed.
  • There is a reasonable possibility the requesting party succeeds on the merits, without binding the tribunal later.

Revocation and liability (Arts 23.5, 23.6, 23.7, 23.8, 23.9)

  • Measures may be modified, suspended or terminated on application or, exceptionally, on the tribunal's own initiative with notice.
  • Security may be required from the requesting party, and material changes in circumstances disclosed.
  • If the tribunal later finds the measure should not have been granted, the requesting party may be liable for costs and damages, awardable at any point.
  • Applying to a court for interim relief is not incompatible with the arbitration agreement.

Security for costs (Art 24)

  • The tribunal may make an order requiring a party to provide security for the costs of the arbitration.
  • This is distinct from security in connection with an interim measure under Art 23.6, which is directed at the party requesting the measure.
  • The Rules set out no express test; the tribunal exercises the power in accordance with its general procedural discretion under Art 13.
  • Any third party funding arrangement disclosed under Art 44 is a factor the tribunal may weigh, and is expressly relevant to costs under Art 34.4(d).

Schedule 4: Emergency Relief (Art 23.1, Sch 4)

  • Applies where a party needs urgent interim relief before an arbitral tribunal exists.
  • A party may apply for Emergency Relief before, at the same time as, or after filing the Notice of Arbitration.
  • Only available before the arbitral tribunal is constituted.
  • The arbitration need not have commenced first, provided the Notice follows within 7 days.
  • Applying to a court for interim relief remains compatible with the arbitration agreement.

Paragraph 2. Contents of the Application for Emergency Relief (Sch 4 ¶2)

  • Names and contact information (email, address, phone) of all parties and their representatives.
  • The circumstances giving rise to the Application and to the underlying dispute.
  • A statement of the Emergency Relief sought.
  • Why the relief is required on an urgent basis and cannot await constitution of the tribunal.
  • Why the applicant is entitled to the relief sought.
  • Copies of any relevant agreements, in particular the arbitration agreement.
  • Comments on the language, the seat and the law applicable to the arbitration.
  • Confirmation that the Application Deposit has been paid.
  • Disclosure of any funding agreement and the identity of any third party funder (Art 44).
  • Confirmation that copies have been communicated simultaneously to all other parties, by the means identified.

Appointment, procedure and the Emergency Decision (Sch 4)

  • HKIAC seeks to appoint an emergency arbitrator within 24 hours of receiving both the Application and the Application Deposit; he or she must be and remain impartial and independent.
  • Duty to disclose anything likely to give rise to justifiable doubts, on the same standards as apply to arbitrators under Art 11.
  • Cannot act as an arbitrator in the arbitration itself unless all parties agree.
  • Deposit is set by HKIAC in the amount published on its website as at the date the Application is submitted, comprising HKIAC's emergency administrative fees plus the emergency arbitrator's fees and expenses.
  • The emergency arbitrator is paid at an hourly rate under Schedule 2, capped at the published amount unless the parties agree otherwise or HKIAC decides otherwise in exceptional circumstances.
  • HKIAC may request additional deposits at any time during the proceedings.
  • Failure to pay additional deposits within HKIAC's time limit means the Application is dismissed.
  • Art 11 applies to the emergency arbitrator, except that the time limits in Arts 11.7 and 11.9 are shortened to three days (Sch 4 ¶7): a challenge must be brought within three days, and HKIAC decides within three days unless the arbitrator resigns or the other party agrees.
  • Where an emergency arbitrator dies, is successfully challenged, is otherwise removed or resigns, HKIAC seeks to appoint a substitute within 24 hours, and the proceedings resume where they left off unless the substitute decides otherwise (Sch 4 ¶8).
  • The file is transmitted to the emergency arbitrator once appointed.
  • The emergency arbitrator establishes a procedure suitable to the urgency, having regard to the nature and circumstances of the case.
  • No entitlement to a hearing: the Application may be decided on documents alone, by telephone or by video conference.
  • Each party must be given a reasonable opportunity to be heard.
  • The emergency arbitrator may rule on its own jurisdiction, including on objections to the existence, validity or scope of the arbitration agreement.
  • Without prejudice to the tribunal's later determination of the same question.
  • Has the powers of a tribunal to order interim measures under Art 23.
  • May require the applicant to provide appropriate security.
  • May order the disclosure of material changes in the circumstances relied on.
  • NEW IN 2024: may make preliminary or interim orders at any point before the Emergency Decision itself.
  • Made within 14 days of the date the file was transmitted, unless HKIAC extends the period.
  • In writing, stating the reasons and the date, and signed.
  • Binds the parties when rendered; they must comply without delay.
  • It has the same effect as an interim measure granted under Art 23 and binds the parties when rendered (Sch 4 ¶17); note that Art 2.13 excludes an emergency arbitrator's decision from the definition of an award.
  • May be modified, suspended or terminated by the emergency arbitrator or, once constituted, by the tribunal.
  • Lapses on the tribunal's final award, on withdrawal of all claims or termination of the arbitration, or if no tribunal is constituted within 90 days of the Decision (¶18(d)).
  • NEW IN 2024 (¶13): an emergency arbitrator whose file has passed to the tribunal may nonetheless continue with, and decide, an application already before it, rather than the application simply falling away.
  • Terminates if the Notice of Arbitration is not submitted to HKIAC within 7 days of HKIAC receiving the Application, unless the emergency arbitrator extends that time limit (Sch 4 ¶21).
  • The emergency arbitrator has no further power to act once the tribunal is constituted, subject to ¶13 (Sch 4 ¶14).
  • Terminates if the Application is dismissed for non-payment of an additional deposit.
  • The costs of the Emergency Relief proceedings are fixed by the emergency arbitrator.
  • They form part of the costs of the arbitration under Art 34.1(e).
  • The emergency arbitrator may apportion them, subject to the tribunal's power to reallocate them later.

Emergency arbitrator: the sequence (Sch 4)

  1. Application filed before, with or after the Notice, but before the tribunal is constituted.
  2. Application Deposit paid; HKIAC will not proceed without it.
  3. HKIAC seeks to appoint an emergency arbitrator within 24 hours of receiving both.
  4. File transmitted to the emergency arbitrator, who fixes the procedure and may rule on its own jurisdiction.
  5. Preliminary or interim orders may be made at any point before the Emergency Decision.
  6. Emergency Decision rendered within 14 days of file transmission; it binds on rendering.
  7. Notice of Arbitration must be filed within 7 days of the Application, or the procedure terminates.
  8. The Decision lapses on the tribunal's final award, on termination, or if no tribunal is constituted within 90 days.

Expedited procedure (Art 42)

  • Applied for before constitution, where the amount in dispute is below the published threshold, or the parties agree, or there is exceptional urgency.
  • Referred to a sole arbitrator unless the agreement provides for three, in which case HKIAC invites agreement on a sole arbitrator.
  • HKIAC may shorten time limits; the parties are in principle entitled to one Statement of Claim, one Defence and Counterclaim, and one Defence to Counterclaim.
  • Decided on documents unless the tribunal considers a hearing appropriate; reasons may be in summary form.
  • Award within six months of file transmission, extendable in appropriate circumstances; the procedure may be disapplied at the request of a party or the tribunal.

Early determination (Art 43)

  • Available where a point is manifestly without merit, manifestly outside jurisdiction, or would yield no award even if assumed correct.
  • Request goes to the tribunal, HKIAC and all parties, as promptly as possible.
  • The tribunal decides within 30 days whether to dismiss the request or fix a procedure, and makes its order or award within 60 days of that decision.
  • Pending determination, the tribunal decides whether and how far the arbitration proceeds.

From closure to award: the sequence (Arts 31, 33)

  1. Each party has had a reasonable opportunity to present its case.
  2. The tribunal declares the relevant phase closed, no later than 45 days after the last directed submissions.
  3. No further submissions or evidence unless the tribunal reopens the proceedings.
  4. The tribunal communicates an anticipated award date to the parties and HKIAC.
  5. Award made no later than 3 months from closure, extendable by agreement or by HKIAC.
  6. Majority decides; failing a majority the presiding arbitrator decides alone, and with prior agreement may make procedural rulings alone.
  7. HKIAC affixes its seal and the award is communicated to the parties.

Form and effect (Art 35)

  • Single or separate awards on different issues at different times, in interim, interlocutory, partial or final form.
  • In writing, final and binding on the parties and those claiming through them.
  • Parties waive rights of recourse or defence on setting aside, enforcement and execution, so far as validly waivable, and must comply without delay.
  • Reasons stated unless agreed otherwise; signed and dated, stating the seat; HKIAC affixes its seal.

Applicable law and amiable compositeur (Art 36)

  • The tribunal decides the substance of the dispute in accordance with the rules of law agreed by the parties.
  • A designation of the law of a given jurisdiction is construed, unless otherwise expressed, as referring directly to that jurisdiction's substantive law and not to its conflict of laws rules.
  • Failing any designation by the parties, the tribunal applies the rules of law it determines to be appropriate.
  • The tribunal decides as amiable compositeur or ex aequo et bono only if the parties have expressly agreed that it should.
  • In all cases the tribunal decides in accordance with the terms of the relevant contracts, and may take into account the usages of the trade applicable to the transaction.

After the award: the sequence (Arts 38, 39, 40)

  1. Award received by the parties; the 30-day clock starts for all three applications below.
  2. Correction of clerical or computational errors requested within 30 days; the tribunal may also correct on its own initiative within 30 days of the award.
  3. Interpretation of a specific point requested within 30 days, and given within 30 days of the request.
  4. Additional award for claims presented but omitted requested within 30 days, and made within 60 days of the request.
  5. Any correction, interpretation or additional award forms part of the award and takes the same form.

Settlement and termination (Art 37)

  • Before constitution, a party wishing to terminate notifies the others and HKIAC; absent objection within the time limit HKIAC may terminate, and if there is an objection the arbitration proceeds.
  • After constitution, on settlement the tribunal issues a termination order or records the settlement as an award on agreed terms, without needing reasons.
  • If continuing becomes unnecessary or impossible, the tribunal issues a termination order unless a party raises a justifiable objection.

What counts as costs (Art 34.1, Sch 4 ¶16)

  • Fees of the tribunal.
  • Reasonable travel and other expenses of the tribunal.
  • Reasonable costs of expert advice and other assistance required by the tribunal, including any tribunal secretary.
  • Reasonable costs of legal representation and other assistance, including witness and expert fees, if claimed during the arbitration.
  • Costs of Emergency Relief proceedings under Schedule 4.
  • Registration and Administrative Fees and expenses payable to HKIAC.

Capping and apportionment (Arts 34.2, 34.3, 34.4, 34.6)

  • The tribunal may limit recoverable costs to a specified amount and may apportion all or part of the costs between the parties.
  • Factors include relative success, scale and complexity, the parties' conduct, third party funding, any outcome related fee structure, and adverse environmental impact arising from the parties' conduct.
  • Costs are determined in the text of the order or award, including on termination or an award on agreed terms.

Third party funding disclosure (Art 44)

  • If a funding agreement is made, the funded party must give written notice to all other parties, the tribunal, any emergency arbitrator and HKIAC.
  • The notice must state the fact that a funding agreement has been made, and the identity of the third party funder.
  • For agreements made on or before commencement, notice goes in the emergency arbitrator application, the Notice, the Answer, the Request for Joinder or the Answer to the Request for Joinder, as applicable.
  • For agreements made after commencement, notice must be given as soon as practicable after the agreement is made.
  • Any subsequent change to that information must also be disclosed.

The confidentiality obligation (Art 45, Arts 45.1, 45.2, 45.4)

  • Unless otherwise agreed, no party or party representative may publish, disclose or communicate information relating to the arbitration under the arbitration agreement, or to an award or Emergency Decision made in it.
  • The same obligation applies to the tribunal, any emergency arbitrator, expert, witness, tribunal secretary and HKIAC.
  • The deliberations of the tribunal are confidential.

The five exceptions (Art 45.3)

  • To protect or pursue a legal right or interest of the party, or to enforce or challenge an award or Emergency Decision, in legal proceedings before a court or other authority.
  • To a government body, regulatory body, court or tribunal where the party is obliged by law to make the disclosure.
  • To a professional or other adviser of any of the parties, including any actual or potential witness or expert.
  • To any party or additional party and any confirmed or appointed arbitrator, for the purposes of Arts 27, 28, 29 or 30.
  • To a person for the purposes of having, or seeking, third party funding of the arbitration.

Publication of awards (Art 45.5)

  • HKIAC may publish an award in full, in excerpts or as a summary, only if all references to the parties' names and other identifying information are deleted.
  • And only if no party objects within the time limit fixed by HKIAC for that purpose; if there is an objection, the award is not published.

Information security (Art 45A)

  • The parties may agree any reasonable measures to protect information shared, stored or processed in relation to the arbitration.
  • The tribunal may, after consulting the parties, give directions to protect the security of such information, taking the circumstances of the case into account.
  • The tribunal may, after consulting the parties, make a decision, order or award in respect of any breach of the agreed or directed information security measures.

Exclusion of liability (Art 46)

  • HKIAC's Council members, designated bodies or persons, the Secretary-General and Secretariat staff, the tribunal, any emergency arbitrator, tribunal-appointed expert and tribunal secretary are not liable for any act or omission in connection with an arbitration under the Rules, save where done or omitted dishonestly.
  • Once the award is made and the possibilities of correction, interpretation and additional awards under Arts 38 to 40 have lapsed or been exhausted, none of those persons is obliged to make statements about the arbitration.
  • Nor may a party seek to make any of them a witness in later legal or other proceedings arising out of the arbitration.

Filings and appointment (Arts 4.3, 5.1, 9.3, 9A)

  • Art 4.3(g) and Art 5.1(e): a proposal as to the number of arbitrators must now be a reasoned proposal, in both the Notice and the Answer.
  • Art 9.3: confirmation of a designation must now take into account any factors that may affect the efficiency or integrity of the arbitration.
  • Art 9A: new article on diversity. Parties and co-arbitrators are encouraged to take diversity into account when designating; HKIAC must do so when exercising its own power to appoint.

Conduct and costs (Arts 13.1, 34.4)

  • Art 13.1: in adopting suitable procedures the tribunal must now also have regard to information security and environmental impact, alongside the existing factors of complexity, amount in dispute and effective use of technology.
  • Art 34.4(f): any adverse environmental impact arising out of the parties' conduct in the arbitration is now a factor the tribunal may weigh when determining and apportioning costs.

Deposits and the expedited procedure (Arts 41.4, 42.2, 42.3)

  • Art 41.4(a): where deposits go unpaid and the tribunal is not yet constituted, HKIAC may now suspend or cease to administer the arbitration.
  • Art 42.2(f): the six-month deadline for an expedited award may now be extended in appropriate circumstances, a lower threshold than the exceptional circumstances required under the 2018 Rules.
  • Art 42.3: the expedited procedure may now be disapplied at the request of the arbitral tribunal, not only at the request of a party.

  • Art 45A: entirely new article. Parties may agree reasonable information security measures; the tribunal may direct them after consulting the parties, and may make a decision, order or award on any breach.

  • Sch 4 ¶10: the emergency arbitrator now has express power to make any preliminary or interim order it considers necessary before making the Emergency Decision.
  • Sch 4 ¶13: the emergency arbitrator may now continue the proceedings and make the Emergency Decision within the 14-day period even if the case file has in the meantime been transmitted to the tribunal.

  • The 2024 Rules apply where the Notice of Arbitration is submitted on or after 1 June 2024, unless the parties have agreed otherwise (Art 1.4).

Where categories overlap

Note that some articles belong in more than one prescribed place; categorising them into a single box would mean omitting information. Art 27, in particular, is a commencement step, a document-content provision, a time limit, a fee trigger and a tribunal-constitution provision all in one.

Two obligations are present across almost all filings: confirmation of simultaneous service with identification of the means used, and disclosure of any third party funding (under Art 44).
