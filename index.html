02Required contents of party filings
Art 4.3↗Art 5.1↗Art 5.3↗Art 16↗Art 17↗Art 11.8↗Art 27.6↗Art 28.3↗Art 43.4↗Art 44↗Sch 4 ¶2↗
Notice, Answer, Statement of Claim and Defence, notice of challenge, Requests for Joinder and Consolidation with answers and comments, request for early determination, Application for Emergency Relief. Service confirmation and funding disclosure recur across nearly all of them.
Answer: 30 days. Answer to Joinder and party comments: 15 days. Joinder: no later than the Statement of Defence, absent exceptional circumstances.

Notice of Arbitration (Art 4)

  • Request that the dispute be referred to arbitration.
  • Names and contact details (email, address, phone) of all parties and their representatives.
  • Copy of the arbitration agreements invoked, plus copies of all contracts or other legal instruments related to or referenced in the dispute.
  • General nature of the claim, the amount involved and the relief or remedy sought.
  • Claimant's proposal and any comments regarding the designated arbitrator(s).
  • Disclosure of any funding agreement and the identity of any third party funder.
  • Confirmation that copies of the Notice and supporting materials have been sent to the Respondent simultaneously.
  • Must be accompanied by payment of the Registration Fee; may include the Statement of Claim.

Answer to the Notice (Art 5, within 30 days)

  • Contact information.
  • Any plea objecting that a tribunal under these Rules lacks jurisdiction.
  • Respondent's comments and answer on the nature of the claim, the amount involved and the relief sought.
  • Respondent's proposal and comments regarding the designated arbitrator(s).
  • Disclosure of any funding agreement and third party funder.
  • Confirmation of simultaneous service on the Claimant.
  • May include the Statement of Defence if the Notice included the Statement of Claim.
  • Any counterclaim, set-off defence or cross-claim raised here, with copies of related contracts, a description of the nature and amount, and the relief sought.

Statement of Claim (Art 16)

  • Statement of facts supporting the claim.
  • Points at issue.
  • Legal arguments.
  • Relief or remedy sought.

Statement of Defence (Art 17)

  • Reply to all particulars in the Statement of Claim.
  • If applicable, the legal and factual basis of any objection to jurisdiction or to the proper constitution of the tribunal.
  • If applicable, the same four particulars for a counterclaim, set-off defence or cross-claim.

Request for Joinder (Art 27.6)

  • Case reference; contact information.
  • Request that the additional party be joined.
  • Copies of contracts or legal instruments giving the legal basis of the request.
  • Statement of facts; points at issue; legal arguments; relief sought.
  • Existence of any funding agreement and third party funder.
  • Confirmation that full copies have been communicated to all parties.

Answer to the Request for Joinder (Art 27.7, within 15 days)

  • Contact information.
  • Any plea that the tribunal has been improperly constituted or lacks jurisdiction over the additional party.
  • Comments on the particulars set out in the Request; answer to the relief sought.
  • Any additional claims sought; any funding agreement or funder.
  • Confirmation of communication to all parties.
  • An additional party wishing to join files its own Request; all other parties comment within 15 days.

Request for Consolidation (Art 28.3)

  • Case references; contact information.
  • Request that the arbitrations be consolidated.
  • Copy of the arbitration agreement; copies of related contracts and legal instruments.
  • Description of the nature of each claim and the amount involved.
  • Statement of facts and written consent of all parties to consolidate, where relevant.
  • Points at issue; legal arguments.
  • Details of any mandatory provision affecting consolidation.
  • Comments on constitution of the tribunal if the Request is granted.
  • Confirmation of service; non-requesting parties and arbitrators may be asked for comments.

Request for Early Determination (Art 43.4)

  • Made as promptly as possible.
  • Request and description of the points of law or fact.
  • Statement of facts; legal arguments.
  • Proposed form of the early determination procedure.
  • Comments on how that proposal meets the efficiency and fairness objectives.
  • Confirmation of simultaneous communication.
03Service and communications
Art 3.1↗Art 3.2↗Art 3.3↗Art 3.4↗Art 13.3↗Art 13.7↗Art 13.8↗
Valid addresses and channels including secure repositories and agreed electronic means, the fallback where communication cannot be effected, deemed receipt including with multiple recipients, copying all parties and HKIAC, and notification of representatives and changes to them.
Deemed receipt on the earliest day of communication or upload; with multiple recipients, on receipt by the last intended recipient.
  • Communication is valid to the address, fax or email given by the addressee in the arbitration; failing that, one specified in the parties' agreement; failing that, one the addressee holds out to the world; failing that, any last known address.
  • Upload to an agreed secure online repository, or any other agreed electronic form approved by HKIAC and the tribunal, also counts.
  • Where communication cannot be effected after reasonable efforts, sending to the last known address by a means that records the attempt is treated as receipt.
  • All written communications between a party and the tribunal must be copied to all other parties and HKIAC.
  • Representatives' details go to all parties, HKIAC, any emergency arbitrator and the tribunal; proposed changes after constitution must be communicated promptly.
  • Cross-cutting: nearly every filing carries a confirmation-of-service requirement identifying the means used.
04Time limits and their calculation
Art 3.5↗Art 3.6↗Art 21↗Art 5.1↗Art 6.1↗Art 11.7↗Art 31.1↗Art 41.4↗Art 42.2↗Art 43.5↗Sch 4 ¶12↗
When time starts to run, treatment of holidays and non-business days, HKIAC's power to amend time limits including expired ones, and every deadline in the Rules from filings through constitution, payment, closure, award and post-award applications.
Written statements: 45 days guideline. Closure: within 45 days of last submissions. Award: 3 months from closure. Expedited award: 6 months.

Calculation

  • Time runs from the day after receipt or deemed receipt.
  • If the last day is a holiday or non-business day at the place of receipt, it extends to the next business day; intervening holidays still count.
  • HKIAC may amend its own and the Rules' time limits, including expired ones, but not those agreed by the parties or set by the tribunal unless they agree.

Party filings

  • Answer to the Notice: 30 days.
  • Answer to a Request for Joinder, and other parties' comments: 15 days.
  • Further written statements: should not exceed 45 days; extensions available even after expiry.

Constitution

  • Number of arbitrators: agreed before commencement or within 30 days, else HKIAC decides.
  • Sole arbitrator: 30 days from the Notice if agreed pre-commencement; 15 days if agreed later or decided by HKIAC.
  • Three arbitrators: 15-day windows for each designation; presiding arbitrator within 30 days of the second confirmation.
  • Fee method notified within 30 days; challenge within 15 days.

Closure, award and after

  • Closure no later than 45 days from the last directed submissions.
  • Award within 3 months of closure, extendable by agreement or by HKIAC.
  • Correction, interpretation or additional award: requested within 30 days of receipt; additional award made within 60 days.

Payments and tracks

  • Deposits: 30 days from request.
  • Expedited award: 6 months from file transmission, extendable in appropriate circumstances.
  • Early determination: decision in 30 days, order or award in 60 days.
  • Emergency: appointment sought within 24 hours; decision in 14 days; Notice within 7 days of the Application; decision lapses if no tribunal within 90 days.
05Payments, fees and deposits
Art 4.4↗Art 10↗Art 41↗Art 18.2↗Art 27.15↗Art 28.10↗Sch 1↗Sch 2↗Sch 3↗Sch 4 ¶5↗
Registration Fee, Administrative Fees and calculation of the amount in dispute, deposits, choice between hourly rates and the sum-in-dispute scale, secretary rate cap, hearing expenses, cancellation fees, lien over the award, Application Deposit, and adjustments triggered by procedural steps.
Fee method notified within 30 days. Deposits due within 30 days of request; unpaid before constitution, HKIAC may suspend or cease administering.

Registration Fee (Sch 1 ¶1)

  • Non-refundable, submitted with the Notice, in the amount stated on HKIAC's website; otherwise the arbitration will not proceed.

Administrative Fees (Sch 1 ¶2)

  • In accordance with the website schedule as at the date of the Notice.
  • Claims and counterclaims are added to determine the amount in dispute, as are set-off defences and cross-claims unless the tribunal determines otherwise.
  • Interest claims are not taken into account; with alternative claims only the principal claim is counted.
  • HKIAC may depart from the scheduled fees in exceptional circumstances, and fixes a figure where the amount is unquantified.
  • Parties are jointly and severally liable.

Deposits (Art 41)

  • Claimant and Respondent each put down an equal deposit after the Notice.
  • Separate deposits for counterclaims or cross-claims; supplementary deposits may be required throughout.
  • If unpaid within 30 days, HKIAC informs the parties so one may pay; if still unpaid, the procedure may be suspended, ceased or terminated depending on whether the tribunal is constituted.
  • A party paying for another may request an award for reimbursement.
  • On release of the final award, any unexpended balance is returned in the shares paid, or as the tribunal directs.

Tribunal fees (Art 10, Sch 2 and 3)

  • Either hourly rates under Schedule 2 or the sum-in-dispute scale under Schedule 3, agreed within 30 days of the Notice, defaulting to Schedule 2.
  • Hourly rates run from confirmation to final award or order, must be agreed before confirmation, and cannot exceed the published cap.
  • An arbitrator may increase the agreed rate by up to 10% on each anniversary; higher rates only by agreement of all parties or HKIAC in exceptional circumstances.
  • Travel: 50% of the rate for time travelling but not working, 100% for work done while travelling.
  • Under Schedule 3 the scale figure is the maximum payable, with the same aggregation, interest and alternative-claim rules.
  • Parties are jointly and severally liable regardless of who appointed whom; HKIAC and the tribunal hold a lien over the award.

Cancellation and postponement (Sch 2 ¶10)

  • Requested by the tribunal: no charge.
  • By a party 30 days or less before the first booked day: 75% of eight times the hourly rate, per day.
  • By a party between 30 and 60 days before: 50% of eight times the hourly rate, per day.
  • By a party more than 60 days before: no charge.
  • If the arbitrator worked during booked days, the higher of hourly fees or the cancellation fee applies.
  • Cancellation other than by agreement or at the tribunal's request may be weighed in the later apportionment of costs.

Application Deposit for Emergency Relief (Sch 4 ¶5)

  • Set by HKIAC as published on the date the Application is submitted, comprising HKIAC's emergency administrative fees and the emergency arbitrator's fees and expenses.
  • Payment must be confirmed within the Application itself; HKIAC will not proceed without it.
  • The emergency arbitrator is paid hourly under Schedule 2, capped at the published amount unless the parties agree otherwise or HKIAC decides otherwise in exceptional circumstances.
  • Additional deposits may be requested at any time; failure to pay within HKIAC's time limit means the Application is dismissed.
06Constitution of the tribunal
Art 6↗Art 7↗Art 8↗Art 9↗Art 9A↗Art 11↗Art 12↗Art 27.12↗Art 28.8↗Art 29.2↗
Number and appointment including multiparty scenarios, confirmation and the efficiency and integrity factor, diversity, impartiality and independence, the nationality rule, disclosure duties, ex parte restrictions, challenge and replacement, and deemed waiver of designation rights.
Challenge: 15 days from notification or from learning of the circumstances. Presiding arbitrator: 30 days from confirmation of the second.

Number (Art 6)

  • HKIAC decides one or three if the parties cannot agree before commencement or within 30 days of the Notice.

Sole arbitrator (Art 7)

  • Agreed before commencement: joint designation within 30 days of the Notice.
  • Agreed after commencement: joint designation within 15 days of that agreement.
  • Decided by HKIAC: joint designation within 15 days of receiving the decision, failing which HKIAC appoints.

Three arbitrators (Art 8)

  • Agreed before commencement: one designation each, named in the Notice and the Answer, failing which HKIAC appoints.
  • Agreed after commencement: Claimant designates within 15 days of the agreement, Respondent within 15 days of that designation.
  • The two designate a presiding arbitrator; if not within 30 days of the second confirmation, HKIAC appoints.
  • More than two parties: the same procedure applies, but any failure to designate, or disagreement over the two-sides premise, may result in HKIAC appointing all three.

Confirmation and challenge

  • All designations take effect only on HKIAC confirmation, which considers agreed qualifications, factors affecting efficiency or integrity, impartiality and independence, and availability.
  • Diversity is a stated consideration for parties, co-arbitrators and HKIAC.
  • Notice of challenge within 15 days, stating reasons, communicated to HKIAC, all parties, the challenged arbitrator and the rest of the tribunal.
  • Joinder, consolidation or multi-contract claims trigger a deemed waiver of designation rights and may lead HKIAC to revoke and re-appoint.
07Procedural framework and case management
Art 13.1↗Art 13.2↗Art 13.4↗Art 13.6↗Art 13.10↗Art 13.11↗Art 14↗Art 15↗Art 18↗Art 20↗
Procedural discretion with regard to technology, information security and environmental impact, the provisional timetable, tribunal secretaries, bifurcation and preliminary issues, conflicts from changed representation, seat and venue, language, amendments, and further written statements.
Timetable at an early stage after consulting the parties. Seat defaults to Hong Kong absent agreement or a contrary determination.
  • The tribunal provides a provisional timetable at an early stage, to the parties and HKIAC.
  • It has discretion to dispose of preliminary issues, bifurcate, and conduct the arbitration in sequential stages.
  • HKIAC may revoke the appointment of an arbitrator who fails to comply with the Rules or the prescribed time limits.
  • Parties may pursue other means of settling the dispute after commencement; the arbitration is then suspended on appropriate terms and resumes at any party's request.
  • Claims and defences may be amended so long as the amendment does not fall outside the tribunal's jurisdiction; fees may be adjusted accordingly.
  • Further written statements may be required, with time limits that should not exceed 45 days, extensions available.
08Evidence and hearings
Art 22.1↗Art 22.3↗Art 22.4↗Art 22.5↗Art 25↗
Burden of proof, admissibility and weight, document production, whether to hold hearings and notice of them, examination of witnesses, translation and records, privacy of hearings, and tribunal-appointed experts.
Adequate advance notice of hearing date, time and place. Art 11 applies by analogy to tribunal-appointed experts.
  • Each party bears the burden of proving the facts it relies on; the tribunal determines admissibility, relevance, materiality and weight, including whether to apply strict rules of evidence.
  • The tribunal may request documents and exhibits, and may admit or exclude any evidence.
  • It decides whether hearings are needed or whether the case proceeds on documents alone, and the manner in which a witness or expert is examined.
  • Hearings are private unless the parties agree otherwise.
  • Experts may be appointed after consulting the parties, with terms of reference established by the tribunal, and parties must give them relevant information and documents.
  • Parties may comment on the expert's report, examine the documents relied on, request examination at a hearing, and present their own experts there.
09Default and non-participation
Art 26.1↗Art 26.2↗Art 26.3↗Art 41.4↗Art 32.1↗
Claimant's failure to file and the tribunal's power to terminate, Respondent's failure and the power to proceed, failure to present a case generally, consequences of unpaid deposits, and deemed waiver of the right to object.
Triggered by failure within a time limit set by the tribunal, absent sufficient cause.
  • If the Claimant misses the tribunal's time limit for a written statement without sufficient cause, the arbitration may be terminated, unless another party has brought a claim and wants it to continue.
  • If the Respondent misses the limit, the tribunal may proceed regardless.
  • If a party fails to present its case as directed, the tribunal may proceed and make an award on the evidence before it.
  • Non-payment of deposits allows HKIAC to suspend or cease administering pre-constitution, and the tribunal to suspend, terminate or continue afterwards.
  • A party that knows of non-compliance with the Rules and proceeds without promptly objecting waives its right to object.
10Jurisdiction
Art 19.1↗Art 19.2↗Art 19.3↗Art 19.5↗Art 27.2↗Sch 4 ¶10↗
Competence-competence and separability, timing of pleas, pre-constitution questions and HKIAC's prima facie review, joinder decisions taken without prejudice to jurisdiction, and the emergency arbitrator's power to rule on its own jurisdiction.
Plea in the Answer if possible, and no later than the Statement of Defence, or the Defence to Counterclaim. Later pleas admissible if the delay is justified.
  • The tribunal may rule on its own jurisdiction, including objections to the existence, validity or scope of the arbitration agreement.
  • An arbitration agreement forming part of a contract is treated as independent of the rest, so a finding that the contract is void does not necessarily invalidate it.
  • Designating or participating in the appointment of an arbitrator does not preclude a jurisdictional plea.
  • Questions arising before constitution, including whether claims were properly brought under Art 29, are decided by the tribunal once constituted.
  • The arbitration proceeds only so far as HKIAC is satisfied prima facie that an agreement may exist, without prejudice to admissibility or merits.
11Multiparty and multi-contract mechanisms
Art 27↗Art 28↗Art 29↗Art 30↗Art 34.5↗Art 32.2↗
Joinder of additional parties, consolidation of pending arbitrations, single arbitration under multiple contracts, concurrent proceedings before the same tribunal, costs in consolidated arbitrations, and waiver of enforcement objections.
Consolidation into the first-commenced arbitration unless agreed or HKIAC decides otherwise. Joinder commences against the additional party on receipt of the Request.

Joinder

  • Raised no later than the Statement of Defence, save in exceptional circumstances.
  • Available where the additional party is prima facie bound by the arbitration agreement, or where all parties including that party expressly agree.
  • Commencement against the additional party runs from receipt of the Request.
  • Once joined, all parties waive the right to designate an arbitrator; HKIAC may revoke appointments and appoint the tribunal itself. Fees are adjusted accordingly.

Consolidation

  • Available where the parties agree, or all claims are under the same agreement, or there is a common question of law or fact, related transactions and compatible agreements.
  • Arbitrations consolidate into the first-commenced unless the parties agree or HKIAC decides otherwise.
  • The same waiver and revocation consequences follow as for joinder.

Multiple contracts and concurrent proceedings

  • Claims under different contracts may be brought in one arbitration where a common question arises under each agreement, the relief arises from the same or related transactions, and the agreements are compatible.
  • Two or more arbitrations may run concurrently or consecutively, or one be suspended, where the same tribunal is constituted in each and a common question arises.
  • Parties waive objections to enforcement based on the use of these procedures, so far as such waiver is valid.
12Interim and urgent relief
Art 23.1↗Art 23.2↗Art 23.4↗Art 23.8↗Art 23.9↗Art 24↗Sch 4↗
Route to emergency relief before constitution, interim measures and the test applied, modification and security, disclosure of changed circumstances, liability for measures that should not have been granted, security for costs, and the emergency arbitrator procedure.
Appointment sought within 24 hours. Emergency Decision within 14 days. Notice within 7 days of the Application. Decision lapses if no tribunal within 90 days.

What interim measures can do

  • Maintain or restore the status quo pending determination of the dispute.
  • Require action, or restraint from action, likely to cause current or imminent harm or prejudice to the arbitral process.
  • Preserve assets out of which an award may be satisfied.
  • Preserve relevant and material evidence.

Factors

  • Harm not adequately reparable by damages is likely if the measure is not ordered.
  • That harm substantially outweighs the likely harm to the party against whom the measure is directed.
  • There is a reasonable possibility the requesting party succeeds on the merits, without binding the tribunal later.

Revocation and liability

  • Measures may be modified, suspended or terminated on application or, exceptionally, on the tribunal's own initiative with notice.
  • Security may be required from the requesting party, and material changes in circumstances disclosed.
  • If the tribunal later finds the measure should not have been granted, the requesting party may be liable for costs and damages, awardable at any point.
  • Applying to a court for interim relief is not incompatible with the arbitration agreement.

Schedule 4: Emergency Relief

  • Applies where a party needs urgent interim relief before an arbitral tribunal exists.
  • A party may apply for Emergency Relief before, at the same time as, or after filing the Notice of Arbitration.
  • Only available before the arbitral tribunal is constituted.
  • The arbitration need not have commenced first, provided the Notice follows within 7 days.
  • Applying to a court for interim relief remains compatible with the arbitration agreement.

Paragraph 9. Contents of the Application for Emergency Relief

  • Names and contact information (email, address, phone) of all parties and their representatives.
  • The circumstances giving rise to the Application and to the underlying dispute.
  • A statement of the Emergency Relief sought.
  • Why the relief is required on an urgent basis and cannot await constitution of the tribunal.
  • Why the applicant is entitled to the relief sought.
  • Copies of any relevant agreements, in particular the arbitration agreement.
  • Comments on the language, the seat and the law applicable to the arbitration.
  • Confirmation that the Application Deposit has been paid.
  • Disclosure of any funding agreement and the identity of any third party funder (Art 44).
  • Confirmation that copies have been communicated simultaneously to all other parties, by the means identified.
  • HKIAC seeks to appoint an emergency arbitrator within 24 hours of receiving both the Application and the Application Deposit; he or she must be and remain impartial and independent.
  • Duty to disclose anything likely to give rise to justifiable doubts, on the same standards as apply to arbitrators under Art 11.
  • Cannot act as an arbitrator in the arbitration itself unless all parties agree.
  • Deposit is set by HKIAC in the amount published on its website as at the date the Application is submitted, comprising HKIAC's emergency administrative fees plus the emergency arbitrator's fees and expenses.
  • The emergency arbitrator is paid at an hourly rate under Schedule 2, capped at the published amount unless the parties agree otherwise or HKIAC decides otherwise in exceptional circumstances.
  • HKIAC may request additional deposits at any time during the proceedings.
  • Failure to pay additional deposits within HKIAC's time limit means the Application is dismissed.
  • A party wishing to challenge must do so promptly after the appointment is communicated, or after learning of the relevant circumstances, stating its reasons.
  • HKIAC decides quickly given the compressed timetable; a challenge does not of itself suspend the procedure.
  • The file is transmitted to the emergency arbitrator once appointed.
  • The emergency arbitrator establishes a procedure suitable to the urgency, having regard to the nature and circumstances of the case.
  • No entitlement to a hearing: the Application may be decided on documents alone, by telephone or by video conference.
  • Each party must be given a reasonable opportunity to be heard.
  • The emergency arbitrator may rule on its own jurisdiction, including on objections to the existence, validity or scope of the arbitration agreement.
  • Without prejudice to the tribunal's later determination of the same question.
  • Has the powers of a tribunal to order interim measures under Art 23.
  • May require the applicant to provide appropriate security.
  • May order the disclosure of material changes in the circumstances relied on.
  • NEW IN 2024: may make preliminary or interim orders at any point before the Emergency Decision itself.
  • Made within 14 days of the date the file was transmitted, unless HKIAC extends the period.
  • In writing, stating the reasons and the date, and signed.
  • Binds the parties when rendered; they must comply without delay.
  • Not an award for all purposes, but enforceable as between the parties on the terms of the Rules.
  • May be modified, suspended or terminated by the emergency arbitrator or, once constituted, by the tribunal.
  • Lapses on the tribunal's final award, on withdrawal of all claims or termination of the arbitration, or if no tribunal is constituted within 90 days of the Decision (¶18(d)).
  • NEW IN 2024 (¶13): an emergency arbitrator whose file has passed to the tribunal may nonetheless continue with, and decide, an application already before it, rather than the application simply falling away.
  • Terminates if the Notice of Arbitration is not filed within 7 days of HKIAC receiving the Application, unless HKIAC extends that period.
  • Terminates if the tribunal is constituted before a Decision is made.
  • Terminates if the Application is dismissed for non-payment of an additional deposit.
  • The costs of the Emergency Relief proceedings are fixed by the emergency arbitrator.
  • They form part of the costs of the arbitration under Art 34.1(e).
  • The emergency arbitrator may apportion them, subject to the tribunal's power to reallocate them later.

Emergency arbitrator: the sequence (Sch 4)

  1. Application filed before, with or after the Notice, but before the tribunal is constituted.
  2. Application Deposit paid; HKIAC will not proceed without it.
  3. HKIAC seeks to appoint an emergency arbitrator within 24 hours of receiving both.
  4. File transmitted to the emergency arbitrator, who fixes the procedure and may rule on its own jurisdiction.
  5. Preliminary or interim orders may be made at any point before the Emergency Decision.
  6. Emergency Decision rendered within 14 days of file transmission; it binds on rendering.
  7. Notice of Arbitration must be filed within 7 days of the Application, or the procedure terminates.
  8. The Decision lapses on the tribunal's final award, on termination, or if no tribunal is constituted within 90 days.
13Alternative procedural tracks
Art 42.1↗Art 42.2↗Art 42.3↗Art 43↗
Expedited procedure: the three gateways, the effect on tribunal size, time limits, pleadings and hearings, and disapplication. Early determination: the three grounds, the required contents of the request, and the decision and determination stages.
Expedited award: 6 months, extendable. Early determination: decision within 30 days, order or award within 60 days.

Expedited procedure (Art 42)

  • Applied for before constitution, where the amount in dispute is below the published threshold, or the parties agree, or there is exceptional urgency.
  • Referred to a sole arbitrator unless the agreement provides for three, in which case HKIAC invites agreement on a sole arbitrator.
  • HKIAC may shorten time limits; the parties are in principle entitled to one Statement of Claim, one Defence and Counterclaim, and one Defence to Counterclaim.
  • Decided on documents unless the tribunal considers a hearing appropriate; reasons may be in summary form.
  • Award within six months of file transmission, extendable in appropriate circumstances; the procedure may be disapplied at the request of a party or the tribunal.

Early determination (Art 43)

  • Available where a point is manifestly without merit, manifestly outside jurisdiction, or would yield no award even if assumed correct.
  • Request goes to the tribunal, HKIAC and all parties, as promptly as possible.
  • The tribunal decides within 30 days whether to dismiss the request or fix a procedure, and makes its order or award within 60 days of that decision.
  • Pending determination, the tribunal decides whether and how far the arbitration proceeds.
14Awards and post-award steps
Art 31↗Art 33↗Art 35↗Art 36↗Art 37↗Art 38↗Art 39↗Art 40↗
Closure and reopening, majority decisions and procedural rulings, form and signature and reasons and finality, HKIAC's seal, applicable law and amiable compositeur, settlement and termination, and correction, interpretation and additional awards.
Applications to correct, interpret or supplement: 30 days from receipt of the award. Additional award within 60 days of the request.

From closure to award: the sequence

  1. Each party has had a reasonable opportunity to present its case.
  2. The tribunal declares the relevant phase closed, no later than 45 days after the last directed submissions.
  3. No further submissions or evidence unless the tribunal reopens the proceedings.
  4. The tribunal communicates an anticipated award date to the parties and HKIAC.
  5. Award made no later than 3 months from closure, extendable by agreement or by HKIAC.
  6. Majority decides; failing a majority the presiding arbitrator decides alone, and with prior agreement may make procedural rulings alone.
  7. HKIAC affixes its seal and the award is communicated to the parties.

Form and effect (Art 35)

  • Single or separate awards on different issues at different times, in interim, interlocutory, partial or final form.
  • In writing, final and binding on the parties and those claiming through them.
  • Parties waive rights of recourse or defence on setting aside, enforcement and execution, so far as validly waivable, and must comply without delay.
  • Reasons stated unless agreed otherwise; signed and dated, stating the seat; HKIAC affixes its seal.

After the award: the sequence

  1. Award received by the parties; the 30-day clock starts for all three applications below.
  2. Correction of clerical or computational errors requested within 30 days; the tribunal may also correct on its own initiative within 30 days of the award.
  3. Interpretation of a specific point requested within 30 days, and given within 30 days of the request.
  4. Additional award for claims presented but omitted requested within 30 days, and made within 60 days of the request.
  5. Any correction, interpretation or additional award forms part of the award and takes the same form.

Settlement and termination (Art 37)

  • Before constitution, a party wishing to terminate notifies the others and HKIAC; absent objection within the time limit HKIAC may terminate, and if there is an objection the arbitration proceeds.
  • After constitution, on settlement the tribunal issues a termination order or records the settlement as an award on agreed terms, without needing reasons.
  • If continuing becomes unnecessary or impossible, the tribunal issues a termination order unless a party raises a justifiable objection.
15Costs
Art 34.1↗Art 34.2↗Art 34.3↗Art 34.4↗Art 34.6↗Sch 4 ¶16↗
The closed definition of costs, the power to cap recoverable legal costs, apportionment and its factors, costs on termination, and costs of emergency relief proceedings.
Determined in one or more orders or awards; fixed in the text of any termination order or award on agreed terms.

What counts as costs

  • Fees of the tribunal.
  • Reasonable travel and other expenses of the tribunal.
  • Reasonable costs of expert advice and other assistance required by the tribunal, including any tribunal secretary.
  • Reasonable costs of legal representation and other assistance, including witness and expert fees, if claimed during the arbitration.
  • Costs of Emergency Relief proceedings under Schedule 4.
  • Registration and Administrative Fees and expenses payable to HKIAC.

Capping and apportionment

  • The tribunal may limit recoverable costs to a specified amount and may apportion all or part of the costs between the parties.
  • Factors include relative success, scale and complexity, the parties' conduct, third party funding, any outcome related fee structure, and adverse environmental impact arising from the parties' conduct.
  • Costs are determined in the text of the order or award, including on termination or an award on agreed terms.
16Confidentiality, disclosure and information security
Art 44↗Art 45↗Art 45A↗Art 46↗
Third party funding disclosure and updates, the confidentiality obligation and its exceptions, conditions for HKIAC publishing awards, information security measures and remedies, and exclusion of liability.
Funding disclosed in the relevant filing, or as soon as practicable if agreed after commencement. Changes disclosed as they occur.
  • Funding disclosure is mandatory: the existence of the agreement and the funder's identity, given in the emergency application, Notice, Answer, Request for Joinder or Answer to Joinder as applicable.
  • Confidentiality binds parties, representatives, the tribunal, emergency arbitrators, experts, witnesses, secretaries and HKIAC.
  • Exceptions cover protecting or pursuing a legal right, enforcing or challenging an award, legal obligations to a regulator or court, advisers and witnesses, parties involved in Arts 27 to 30 procedures, and seeking third party funding.
  • HKIAC may publish an award only with identifying information removed and no party objecting within the time limit set.
  • Parties may agree information security measures; the tribunal may direct them and make a decision, order or award on any breach.
  • HKIAC, the tribunal, emergency arbitrators, experts and secretaries are excluded from liability save for dishonesty, and may not be made witnesses in later proceedings.

Where categories overlap

Note that some articles belong in more than one prescribed place; categorising them into a single box would mean omitting information. Art 27, in particular, is a commencement step, a document-content provision, a time limit, a fee trigger and a tribunal-constitution provision all in one.

Two obligations are present across almost all filings: confirmation of simultaneous service with identification of the means used, and disclosure of any third party funding (under Art 44).
